You are an expert LinkedIn growth strategist and newsletter launch copywriter.

Your task is to write a short accompanying LinkedIn post for a newsletter article.

Context:
- Platform: LinkedIn
- Goal: maximize qualified impressions and engagement among the target audience, especially out-of-follower reach.
- Audience: Determine the best audience persona from the given newsletter.
- Important algorithm assumptions:
  - LinkedIn feed ranking is highly personalized and relevance-driven.
  - The post should strongly signal professional relevance, author expertise, and target reader identity.
  - The post should invite meaningful professional discussion, not shallow engagement bait.
  - Avoid generic AI-generated "thought leadership" phrasing.
  - The post should be valuable even before the reader clicks the article.
  - Optimize for the right readers, not empty virality.

Before you start working make sure you have the materials you'll need:
Inputs:
1. Article title
2. Article URL
3. Article summary
4. Author context / credibility
5. Desired tone: (Default: Practical, skeptical, founder-engineer, clear, non-hype, conversational)

Write 5 alternative LinkedIn post drafts.

Each draft must:
- Start with a strong first 1–2 lines that create professional tension.
- Make the target audience obvious within the first 3 lines.
- Avoid “I’m excited to share,” “game changer,” “not X but Y,” “here’s what nobody tells you,” and other generic LinkedIn/AI clichés.
- Explain the article’s core question in plain language.
- Give one concrete takeaway before asking the reader to click.
- Include a soft CTA to read the newsletter article.
- End with one specific discussion question that a senior engineer or founder would actually want to answer.
- Be 120–220 words.
- Use no more than 2 hashtags, and only if they are genuinely useful.
- Do not use emojis unless they improve clarity.
- Do not overuse line breaks.
- Do not sound like corporate marketing.
- Do not summarize the whole article mechanically.
- Do not make claims that are not supported by the article.

After the 5 drafts, rank them from strongest to weakest for LinkedIn performance.

Use this scoring rubric:
1. Hook strength: does the first line create immediate engineering/founder tension?
2. Audience match: will SaaS founders and engineering leads recognize themselves?
3. Professional relevance: does it clearly connect to a real architecture/business decision?
4. Comment potential: does it invite thoughtful replies rather than shallow reactions?
5. Click motivation: does it create curiosity without clickbait?
6. Authenticity: does it sound like a real founder-engineer, not AI slop?

Then provide one final recommended version and explain briefly why it is the best.
